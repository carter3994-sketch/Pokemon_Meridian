## Aevor settlements, ancient history, and Guardians — APPROVED
1. **Two expedition waves:** The first expedition reached Aevor **a couple of years** before the player's expedition, established contact and relations with the native population, and began building permanent settlements. The player arrives with a later expedition.
2. **Permanent cities and headquarters:** Landing Camp is an **already operational expedition HQ** with fully functioning buildings; it is not a temporary encampment the player builds on arrival. Existing approved camp concept art remains authoritative for its visual design.
3. **Two architectural layers:** Cities are functional modern settlements with homes, research buildings, markets, Pokémon Centers, Gyms, government buildings and other civic infrastructure **intermingled with ancient Aevorian remains**. Weathered foundations, collapsed temples, carvings, broken arches, monuments and overgrown paths should appear *throughout* settlement areas, not only in isolated ruins. Some old structures may be incorporated into modern construction. The ancient past remains visually present throughout the game.
4. **Guardian habitats:** Settlers **chose city sites themselves**, deliberately building near or around the habitats of ancient Guardians that watch over the region.
5. **Native warnings:** Native communities warned the first settlers that these locations could cause serious problems in the future. Settlers nevertheless proceeded. Motivations can differ across individuals; neither population should be portrayed as uniformly villainous. *The exact reasons sites were chosen are not yet finalized.*
6. **Gyms and Guardians are distinct:** Major cities keep traditional Pokémon **Gyms** and badge-focused progression. **Guardians** are separate, ancient, lore-linked beings whose confrontations are **inevitable through the main story**, not merely optional side quests.
7. **Encounter variety:** Guardian conflicts should not be locked to an identical “Gym, then Guardian battle” pattern in every city. Timing, stakes, and kind of confrontation may differ; exact encounters remain to be designed and approved.
8. **Storytelling through place:** Architecture should constantly express the tension between settlers' modern expansion and older Aevorian civilization, so exploration communicates lore without relying only on dialogue.
9. **Early introduction:** The rival's father, responsible for aspects of Johto–native relations, can introduce this background while guiding the player through Expedition HQ, drawing attention to ancient remains and native objections. The **specific tour dialogue and his personal beliefs/secrets remain unwritten and unapproved**.
